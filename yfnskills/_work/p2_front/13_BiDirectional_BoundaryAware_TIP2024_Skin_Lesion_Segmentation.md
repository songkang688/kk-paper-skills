# A Bi-Directionally Fused Boundary Aware Network for Skin Lesion Segmentation

**Paper_ID:** 13_BiDirectional_BoundaryAware_TIP2024_Skin_Lesion_Segmentation
**Authors:** Feiniu Yuan (Senior Member, IEEE), Yuhuan Peng, Qinghua Huang, and Xuelong Li (Fellow, IEEE)
**Venue:** IEEE Transactions on Image Processing, 2024. DOI: 10.1109/TIP.2024.3482212

## Abstract

It is quite challenging to visually identify skin lesions with irregular shapes, blurred boundaries and large scale variances. Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) extracts more local features with abundant spatial information, while Transformer has the powerful ability to capture more global information but with insufficient spatial details. To overcome the difficulties in discriminating small or blurred skin lesions, we propose a Bi-directionally Fused Boundary Aware Network (BiFBA-Net). To utilize complementary features produced by CNNs and Transformers, we design a dual-encoding structure. Different from existing dual-encoders, our method designs a Bi-directional Attention Gate (Bi-AG) with two inputs and two outputs for crosswise feature fusion. Our Bi-AG accepts two kinds of features from CNN and Transformer encoders, and two attention gates are designed to generate two attention outputs that are sent back to the two encoders. Thus, we implement adequate exchanging of multi-scale information between CNN and Transformer encoders in a bi-directional and attention way. To perfectly restore feature maps, we propose a progressive decoding structure with boundary aware, containing three decoders with six supervised losses. The first decoder is a CNN network for producing more spatial details. The second one is a Partial Decoder (PD) for aggregating high-level features with more semantics. The last one is a Boundary Aware Decoder (BAD) proposed to progressively improve boundary accuracy. Our BAD uses residual structure and Reverse Attention (RA) at different scales to deeply mine structural and spatial details for refining lesion boundaries. Extensive experiments on public datasets show that our BiFBA-Net achieves higher segmentation accuracy, and has much better ability of boundary perceptions than compared methods. It also alleviates both over-segmentation of small lesions and under-segmentation of large ones.

**Index Terms:** Skin lesion segmentation, deep learning, CNN, transformer, attention.

## Introduction

### I. Introduction

Melanoma is a common skin tumor of high malignancy caused by abnormal melanocyte hyperplasia, accounting for a large proportion of skin tumor deaths. Research has shown that it has become one of the fastest-growing tumors in incidence rates [1]. Dermoscopy is a non-invasive imaging technique for detecting and diagnosing dermatopathies like melanoma, and it is often considered as the golden standard for localizing skin lesions in the early stages. Traditionally, malignant skin lesions can be manually labeled and reviewed by experienced dermatologists, but the process is exceedingly cumbersome and time-consuming. Therefore, automatic skin lesion segmentation in Computer Aided Diagnosis (CAD) has been widely designed as an assisted method to achieve accurate segmentation and assist dermatologists in analyzing malignant dermatopathies.

In general, a typical CAD system for automatic skin lesion segmentation consists of four steps: image preprocessing, segmentation of skin lesions, image feature extraction, and image classification. As an essential step, image segmentation generates a binary mask map by classifying each pixel as part of the object or background classes. As patient-specific properties may vary in skin colors, textures, lesion sizes and shapes, automatic segmentation methods are required to identify such variances. There are numerous artifacts, such as body hairs, reflections, air bubbles, shadows, non-uniform lighting and markers [2], requiring automatic segmentation methods to be robust against shape and scale variations of lesions. In early stages, segmenting skin melanoma from dermoscopy images usually relies on traditional methods, which are roughly based on two categories of technologies: (1) edge detection and thresholding methods [2], and (2) active contour models [3]. These techniques usually consider edge information as a critical factor and therefore mainly focus on extracting skin lesion borders, which depends heavily on the pre-definition of image features and threshold parameters. With the rapid development of deep learning, CNN based methods have widely been exploited, and these deep learning methods have significantly improved segmentation performance in an end-to-end way. Full Convolutional Network (FCN) [4] is a pioneering work of deep learning for semantic segmentation. It classifies each pixel by abstract features automatically extracted by the network. In fact, image segmentation is a pixel-wise classification extended from image level to pixel level. As a variant of FCNs, U-Net [5] was proposed to solve the problem of medical segmentation tasks. It uses a symmetrical encoder-decoder architecture for extracting contextual features and localization information, and it can be trained on small datasets. Later, an improved architecture named U-Net++ [6] was proposed by redesigning a series of nested dense skip connections that integrate features from encoding and decoding layers. These dense skip connections enhance the capture of multi-level features and help reduce scale differences between feature maps during fusion. In addition, other encoder-decoder architectures, such as VNet [7], 3D U-Net [8], Residual U-Net [9], Double U-Net [10], etc., have exhibited excellent performance in multiple medical image segmentation tasks with different imaging modalities. Similarly, a Multi-Stage U-Net (MS-UNet) [11] adopts a deep supervision mechanism and the Jaccard distance loss to supervise every stage, and these techniques makes it more discriminative and perceptive to skin lesions.

However, for the above-mentioned methods, the encoding process inevitably leads to losses of local details and global context information, and the consecutive decoding operations are difficult to fully recover the low-level detailed spatial information that is very important for skin lesion segmentation. Learning long-range dependencies between lesion pixels and surrounding healthy skin ones in medical images plays an essential role in improving the accuracy of segmentation.

The Transformer model [12] uses self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies. Transformer based methods have achieved great successes in Natural Language Processing (NLP). To model long-distance dependencies, Wang et al. [13] presented a non-local network to obtain global features. It bridges self-attention mechanisms to the more general situations of non-local filtering operations, which are applicable to vision tasks by computing information interactions between any two positions. ViT [14] further validates the feasibility of applying Transformers directly to computer vision tasks with some modifications. It splits each image into patches, and linearly transforms each patch to produce patch embeddings. Embedding vectors are fed to a standard Transformer encoder to extract the visual representations. Multihead Self-Attention (MSA) in ViT [14] uses matrix transformations to extract long-distance feature dependencies, which is particularly helpful for downstream tasks. However, it is quite difficult to make a tradeoff between classification accuracy and object localization, since local features are beneficial to localize objects, and global one to classify pixels. Most methods focus on combining local features and global representations.

Dermatologists roughly localize the skin lesion, and then gradually outline the fine mask with detailed spatial features. Inspired by the diagnostic routine, we also utilize boundary cues and scale information to extract boundary-aware and scale-invariant knowledge for progressively improving the accuracy of predicted maps. Based on this idea, we propose a Bi-directionally Fused Boundary Aware Network (BiFBA-Net) that fully mines boundary and scale information. In the BiFBA-Net, we first design a dual-encoder structure with CNNs and Transformer branches that are constructed by ResNet [15] and ViT [14] for simultaneously capturing both local features and global context information. Then, we propose a Bi-directional Attention Gate (Bi-AG) to bi-directionally align Transformer and CNN features, which is factually an effective fusion module. The Bi-AG module is repeatedly used to fuse features from the CNN and Transformer encoders at different scales, thus we implement multi-scale information interactions between local and global features. To fully restore spatial details and obtain global contextual semantics, we design three progressive upsampling decoders, including a CNN decoder, a boundary aware decoder, and a partial decoder. In this manner, our network first locates skin lesions roughly, and then adopt reverse attention modules to mine boundary cues for refining segmentations. Finally, we propose a deep supervision strategy on medium and final outputs for model training. The proposed method demonstrates excellent performance on public skin lesion datasets, including ISIC 2016, ISIC 2017, ISIC 2018 and PH2 datasets. The main contributions of our method are as follows:

1) We propose a dual-encoding structure with a bi-directional feature fusion strategy, which includes a CNN encoder and a Transformer one to extract complementary features. To fully exchange information between CNN and Transformer encoders, we design a Bi-directional Attention Gate (Bi-AG) with two inputs and two outputs. Our Bi-AG accepts two inputs respectively from CNN and Transformer encoders, and then it produces two outputs that are respectively fed back to the two encoders. Thus, we implement highly efficient and adequate feature fusion in a bi-directional and attention way.

2) To adequately restore spatial details and generate contextual semantics, we propose a progressive decoding structure with boundary aware that contains three decoders. A CNN decoder is responsible for capturing more spatial details, a Partial Decoder (PD) is used to aggregate more high-level information for improving semantic confidences, and a Boundary Aware Decoder (BAD) is dedicated to improving the accuracy of segmented boundaries.

3) We propose a Bi-directionally Fused Boundary Aware Network (BiFBA-Net) by integrating a dual-encoding structure with bi-directional information exchanging, a progressive decoding structure with boundary aware, and a deep supervision scheme with six medium losses. The six losses mainly supervise two predictions of CNN and partial decoders, and one final map and three medium feature maps of BAD.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II describes related work on skin lesion segmentation and hybrid networks. Section III describes the proposed method in details. Extensive experiments are given in Section IV. At last, we conclude the paper in Section V.

## RelatedWork

### II. Related Works

#### A. Skin Lesion Segmentation

Traditional methods for skin lesion segmentation heavily rely on low-level hand-crafted features [17], such as edge, color, texture, and so on. Histogram-based thresholding methods [18] have been proposed to produce segmentation maps by extracting color distributions. The image thresholding method [19] utilizes the Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) algorithm to select the optimal threshold level. Celebi et al. [20] presented to first perform automatic skin lesion border detection and make a further step to collect the texture, color features of skin lesions. However, it is challenging for traditional methods to obtain accurate segmentations of skin lesions, since manually designed features may not be suitable for the problem of segmentation.

To improve accuracy, segmentation neural networks have been proposed to automatically extract features and complete classifications in an end-to-end manner. Sarker et al. [21] proposed a robust deep U-shape network to learn an encoder-decoder framework for more precise segmentation of skin lesion boundaries. To achieve satisfactory skin cancer segmentation results, Zhang et al. [22] proposed a deep supervised multi-scale network, which uses side-output layers to aggregate information from shallow and deep layers, and designs a multi-scale connection block to handle a variety of skin cancer sizes. In [23], varying shapes and sizes of objects are automatically learned by designing a novel soft-attention. Esfahani et al. [24] designed a fully convolutional network to produce dense feature maps and used dense pooling layers for improving the accuracy of lesion segmentations. Xie et al. [25] proposed a mutual bootstrapping model for automated skin lesion segmentation and classification (MB-DCNN), which transfers localization information and coarse lesion masks to provide a prior bootstrapping. Lei et al. [26] adopted a dual discrimination strategy to present an effective GAN for enhancing contextual information and improving the boundary accuracy of objects. These above-mentioned methods have achieved encouraging results, but they cannot capture local and global information well.

#### B. CNN and Transformer Hybrid Networks

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have widely been adopted to create encoder-decoder structures [27] for semantic image segmentation. CNN based methods have demonstrated powerful feature representations and pixel-wise classifications. Fang et al. [28] proposed a CNN based image matting network to support stroke-based interactions. The input trimaps are from carefully edited to totally unknown maps. Wang et al. [29] designed a CNN method for urban scene segmentation that is powered by a weakly supervised strategy. CNNs can be used to find dominant information, such as fine character details, for scene text detection and style transfer [30]. Compared to CNNs, Transformers are prone to extract more global information and achieve great success in many tasks [31]. Liu et al. [32] used Transformers to efficiently extract temporal and long-range context information for Temporal Action Detection (TAD).

Combinative methods utilize the individual benefits of different algorithms [33]. Extensive attempts have been made to inherit the inherent structured advantages of both CNNs and Transformers for complementing the shortcoming of each other. Schlemper et al. [23] and Yuan et al. [34] inserted self-attention mechanisms into CNNs to model long-range semantics. It's also prevalent to integrate CNNs and Transformers together to obtain powerful feature representations. Carion et al. [35] proposed an object detection method with Transformers (DETR) [35], where a CNN backbone followed by an encoder-decoder architecture of Transformers is used to extract compact representations. Peng et al. [36] proposed an object recognition and detection algorithm (Conformer), which has a great potential to be a general backbone network due to its merit in coupling local features and global representations. Valanarasu et al. [37] explored a Transformer-based solution to medical image segmentation tasks on smaller datasets, which extends existing architectures by adding an additional control mechanism to self-attention. Other variants of combining CNNs and Transformers have been proposed to handle multi-modality and 3D medical image segmentation tasks [38], [39]. Recently, CTC-Net [40] was presented to extract and fuse the complementary features in Transformer and CNN domains for medical image segmentations. It uses Swin Transformer [41] and ResNet [15] to form a dual encoder, and designs a feature complementary module (FCM) for further improving feature representations. A Feature-Adaptive transformer Network (FAT-Net) [42] was presented to address blurred boundaries of skin lesions. It integrates an extra transformer branch into the classical encoder-decoder architecture and utilizes a memory-efficient decoder as well as a feature adaptation module.

Other methods adopt an ensemble method to utilize the results of different networks [43]. This kind of approaches does not fuse features extracted by Transformers, CNNs or other architectures. To some extents, these methods may achieve better results, since large models often train a set of relatively small networks.
